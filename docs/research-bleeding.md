## Findings

**Red (rubra) stage**
- Cleveland Clinic: "Lochia rubra typically lasts at least three to four days after childbirth."
- MSD consumer: "For 3 to 4 days after delivery, a woman has vaginal bleeding and may pass blood clots."
- MSD professional: "Vaginal discharge is grossly bloody (lochia rubra) for 3 to 4 days"
- All three say 3 to 4 days. D2:L5 says "3 días"; D2:L14 says "3–4 días".

**Later stages and total**
- Cleveland Clinic: serosa "for the fourth through 12th day"; alba "starts around day 12 and can last up to six weeks"; "lochia typically lasts up to six weeks"; "Some people have traces of lochia for up to eight weeks."
- MSD consumer: serosa "lasts for about 14 days"; alba "lasts for up to 14 days"; "Overall, vaginal discharge may continue for up to 6 weeks after delivery."
- MSD professional: after rubra "after the next 10 to 12 days, it changes to yellowish white (lochia alba)."
- D2:L5 and D2:L14 say pink/brown "durante 2–4 semanas". No source above states 2–4 weeks for the pink/brown stage; they give about 14 days for it and up to 6 weeks for the total.

**When to call (as the sources word it)**
- Pad soaking: Cleveland Clinic "soaking a pad every hour"; MSD consumer "blood soaks a sanitary pad every hour for more than 2 hours"; MSD professional "soaking a pad or tampon every 1 to 2 hours". D2:L7 and D2:L14 say "más de 1 toalla por hora".
- Clots: Cleveland Clinic "Large, golf-ball-sized clots"; MSD consumer "larger than a golf ball"; MSD professional "large (> 2.5 cm) blood clots and/or feeling faint". D2 uses "pelota de golf". The sources do not agree with each other on size.
- Bleeding that comes back: Cleveland Clinic "shouldn't completely stop and then start again". D2 matches in substance.
- Red bleeding lasting long: Cleveland Clinic "having lochia rubra (red, bloody discharge) for more than one week may be a red flag." Not in D2.
- Foul smell: MSD consumer "If discharge smells foul". D2:L7 has it.
