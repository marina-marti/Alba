# Grounding for the weekly pelvic floor routine (T-009)

Statements copied from docs/research-pelvic-floor.md (compiled 2026-11-06). This is all the routine prompt may rely on. The sources differ in places; nobody has checked this with a clinician. No source gives a week-by-week pelvic floor schedule.

## Starting
- POGP: "You can commence breathing exercises and pelvic floor exercises within the first few hours after birth (if you had a Caesarean-section, you should wait for the catheter to be removed first)."
- Northern Devon: "As soon as possible after your baby's birth, start these exercises, even if you have stitches." and "If you have a catheter, it is advisable to wait until it is removed and you are passing urine normally before starting pelvic floor exercises."
- Gloucestershire: "We recommend starting the exercises gently the day after you have given birth."
- Milton Keynes: "You can start these exercises from day 1 if you feel ready, but it is important to start gently and build up slowly."
- Sources differ on the start time (first hours, day after, day 1, as soon as possible, after the catheter is out).

## How to do them and how often
- NHS: "Squeeze around your vagina and bladder (urethra) as if you're stopping the flow of urine or squeezing during intercourse." "It's important to keep breathing normally while you do these exercises." "Make sure you do not pull in your stomach when you squeeze." "Long squeezes - hold for as long as you can, but no longer than 10 seconds, then relax." "Aim to build up to 10 repeats of each exercise, at least 3 times a day." "You can do these exercises lying down, sitting or standing."
- POGP: "You should do your PFM exercises at least 3 times a day. Starting in lying and sitting positions and progressing to standing and active positions such as walking and bending." "Do not hold your breath. Do not clench your buttocks." "If you are sore try to do them lying on your side".
- Northern Devon: "Hold at the top of the 'lift' for up to 10 seconds. Release and rest for five seconds." "Repeat as many times as you can (up to 10)." "Pull the muscles in quickly and let go again. Repeat 10 times." "Practise these exercises at least three or four times a day."
- Milton Keynes: "To begin, aim to hold the squeeze for 5 seconds."
- Somerset: "If 8 times is too hard, start with 4 or 6 times." "Rest between each one until your muscles feel tired."
- POGP, the knack: "Draw up and tighten your pelvic floor muscles before any activity that increases the intra-abdominal pressure (coughing/ sneezing/ laughing/ bending)".
- Gloucestershire: "we advise not to do your exercises while emptying your bladder"
- Sources differ on frequency (3 times a day, 3 or 4 times a day) and hold length (5 seconds to begin, up to 10 seconds).

## What to avoid early
- NHS: "Try to avoid exercises such as sit-ups, planks and high-impact exercises to start with." "Also avoid straining on the toilet and heavy lifting."
- POGP: "try to avoid any activity that causes strain for the first few weeks e.g. prolonged standing, vacuuming, carrying heavy objects or bags" and "try not to lift anything heavier than your baby for at least 6 weeks."
- Northern Devon: "Never attempt double leg lifts, as this puts too much strain on your tummy and pelvic floor muscles and could injure your back."
- Oxford: "None of these exercises should cause pain or discomfort, so please ease off or stop any exercises that do."

## Week bands the sources give (rough guides, not targets)
- Oxford, 0-2 weeks after birth: "Pelvic floor muscle exercises." "Walking - increasing distance and speed incrementally as your body feels comfortable."
- Oxford, 2-4 weeks: "Progress your pelvic floor muscle exercises."
- Oxford, 4-6 weeks: "Continue to progress your exercise duration / intensity from previous weeks."
- POGP: "gradually build up your exercise tolerance over the first 6-8 weeks." "Gradually increase the time and pace of your walking every day during the first 6 weeks".
- POGP, 6-12 weeks: "progressing your pelvic floor exercises and strength and conditioning." "*Weekly ranges are suggested as a rough guide and progress should be individual and based on symptoms, healing and how you are feeling".
- POGP: "At your 6-8 week check with your GP, you should be reviewed for general physical and mental wellbeing. If you can, this would be a good time to have a specialist Pelvic Health Physiotherapy assessment".
- NHS Lanarkshire: at 6 weeks "It is important to progress your exercise slowly".
- Northern Devon: "It takes three to six months to strengthen pelvic-floor muscles with regular training." "Just like any other muscle in the body, the pelvic-floor muscles will go weak again if not exercised regularly."
- Somerset: "It might take up to three months for your muscles to get strong again."
- Gloucestershire: "We recommend that you continue to exercise the muscles daily for the rest of your life."
- Sources differ on when stronger exercise is suitable; Oxford, Northern Devon and Gloucestershire say about 12 weeks or three months for running, aerobics or high-impact exercise.

## When to stop and speak to a clinician
- Gloucestershire: "Please ensure if you have any pain, bulging of the abdominals, incontinence or heaviness in the vagina, please stop and speak to your healthcare professional"
- POGP: "If you find this difficult or feel a vaginal bulging when you try to tighten your pelvic floor muscles - get help from a specialist physiotherapist." and "If you are having difficulty passing urine or you have any altered sensation of the need to go, it is important that you tell your doctor immediately."
- Oxford: not to experience "A heaviness or dragging sensation in the vaginal area" or "Pain anywhere in your body" during exercise.
- NHS: "If a physical problem is bothering you, ask a GP or health visitor for help at any time."
- Recovery differs by person and by how the birth went.
